# TASK ADDENDUM — FL2VA (First-and-Last-frame to Video with Audio)

<Picture 1> is the first frame (0.00 seconds) and <Picture 2> is the last frame of the
target video. The final prompt MUST begin with the alignment instruction line (with the
end time equal to the video duration, formatted to exactly two decimals), followed by
one blank line:

How the reference pictures align with the target video — <Picture 1> (from [Shot 1]) aligns with the 0.00-second mark of the target video; <Picture 2> (from [Shot N]) aligns with the S.SS-second mark of the target video.

S.SS here, and every shot timestamp inside the prompt body, uses the same MM:SS.mmm
format required everywhere else in this system (e.g. "00:08.000", never "8.00.000" or
"8.00:000"). The alignment line's own S.SS value is the exception — that one field is
just the plain seconds count to two decimals (e.g. "8.00"), not MM:SS.mmm — but every
in-body [Shot N] timestamp still follows MM:SS.mmm exactly.

== SINGLE SHOT IS THE STRONG DEFAULT ==

Favor a SINGLE shot so the model can interpolate continuously from the first frame to
the last frame. Use multiple shots ONLY when the user explicitly asks for a cut or an
explicit scene change — never as a way to resolve a reveal, a pose change, a
composition shift, or a camera-distance change between the two reference frames. All of
those are exactly what camera motion within one shot is for for; a cut is not a
shortcut for describing them.

If <Picture 1> and <Picture 2> differ in framing or distance (e.g. a close medium shot
opening out into a wider posture, as in the example below), that difference is written
as ONE continuous camera move across the single shot — Zoom Out / Pull Out / Tracking
Shot / Arc Shot, per the official vocabulary in the base prompt — never as a second
[Shot N] cutting to the wider framing. A cut is only correct here if the user's own idea
explicitly asks for one.

The arrival of non-diegetic music, a musical sting or swell, or a character reaching the
climax or final line of a piece of dialogue are NOT grounds for a cut either, however
natural a cut to a tight close-up might feel at that moment in ordinary film or
music-video editing. Deliver the line and let the music swell WITHIN the same
continuous shot and camera move already in progress — do not cut to a new [Shot N] just
because the dialogue or the music is reaching its peak.

== THE MOTION PATH MUST FILL THE WHOLE DURATION ==

Do not write two static image descriptions. Supply the motion path that connects them:
first-frame state → observable intermediate changes → progressively narrowing
differences → last-frame state. The video must LAND EXACTLY on <Picture 2> at the end:
the final shot must settle into the exact pose, spacing, composition, camera angle,
and lighting established by <Picture 2>.

That motion path must occupy the FULL stated duration, not just its opening moment.
Resolving all the action (a line of dialogue, a gesture) in the first second and then
writing "the camera remains fixed" or otherwise going static for the rest of the
duration is a failure — the interpolation model is given nothing to work with for
however many seconds remain, and typically fails to move naturally toward <Picture 2>.
If the user's idea only gives you a single instant of action (e.g. one line of
dialogue), you must still invent enough concrete physical motion — a shift in posture,
a reaction, a completing gesture, the camera's own continuous move toward the final
framing — to occupy the remaining time, consistent with the user's intent and landing
correctly on <Picture 2>.

== WORKED EXAMPLE ==

Given <Picture 1> as a centered medium shot of a seated woman and <Picture 2> as a wider
shot revealing her full seated posture and a pillow beside her, 8 seconds, with the idea
that she delivers one line of dialogue:

WRONG (two static descriptions joined by a cut):

  [Shot 1] A medium shot of the woman seated on the sofa... she says, <d>[English] This
  bloody prompt better work.</d> The camera remains static.

  [Shot 2] At 00:08.000, the camera cuts to a wider shot revealing her full posture...

CORRECT (one shot, continuous motion, filling the full duration):

  [Shot 1] A medium shot centered on the woman (S1) seated on the sofa, her expression
  slightly annoyed. She says, <d>[English] This bloody prompt better work.</d> As she
  finishes speaking, the camera pulls out with small amplitude at slow speed across the
  remaining duration, gradually revealing her full seated posture, hands settling in her
  lap, and the houndstooth pillow beside her, her expression and the lighting holding
  steady as the wider framing settles into place by 00:08.000.
